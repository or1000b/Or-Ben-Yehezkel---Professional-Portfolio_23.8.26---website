Strategic Policy Analysis

Executive Summary: Navigating Israel-Turkey Trade Relations Amidst Sanctions

(Adapted from a Political Economy Policy Paper)

Background

Israel and Turkey have historically been major trade partners under a free trade agreement enacted in 1997. However, during the "Swords of Iron" war, Turkey imposed abrupt economic sanctions on Israel, targeting critical infrastructure materials such as concrete and metals. This paper outlines actionable recommendations for Israeli policymakers to mitigate risks and strategically reposition Israel's trade dependencies.

Actionable Recommendations

Leverage Energy Exports

Israel currently exports ~$570M worth of LNG to Turkey. Israel should gradually cease these exports upon contract expirations and redirect them to European markets experiencing increased demand, such as Greece, Lithuania, or Germany.

Supply Chain Diversification

Secure alternative suppliers for sanctioned goods. For instance, Israel should import copper from the Democratic Republic of Congo (the world's 2nd largest producer) rather than Spain, mitigating reliance on volatile partners.

Strategic Legal Action

Avoid formal litigation at the WTO due to potential biases. Disputes should be handled through non-binding international arbitration mechanisms (similar to ICSID) or informal third-party mediation.

Diplomatic Repositioning

Close the trade attaché in Istanbul to formally signal that Turkey is no longer prioritized as a premier trade partner, and limit operations of foreign associations seeking political influence.

Geopolitical & Intelligence Research

Research Abstract: Turkey's Islamist Foreign Policy and the Strategy of Taqiyya

(Adapted from an Academic Seminar in Middle Eastern Studies)

1 Thesis Overview

Following the rise of the Justice and Development Party (AKP) under Recep Tayyip Erdogan, Turkey has transitioned from a pro-Western, secular state into an active regional power pursuing an Islamist agenda. This research demonstrates that the Turkish government employs the Islamic principle of Taqiyya—a tactic of concealing true intentions and cooperating with stronger powers during times of weakness—as a diplomatic cover to advance its influence under the guise of liberal-democratic values (e.g., through the UN and NATO).

2 Key Case Studies

The Syrian Theater

Turkey capitalized on the Syrian civil war by occupying northern territories, ostensibly to fight ISIS and establish humanitarian safe zones. In reality, Turkey is executing a systematic "Turkification" process. This involves taking direct control of local governance, integrating the Turkish curriculum and language into schools, and supporting Islamist opposition groups to block Kurdish autonomy.

The Libyan Conflict

Turkey militarily intervened in Libya to support the Islamist-leaning Government of National Accord (GNA) against General Khalifa Haftar. While framed as an effort to stabilize the country in alignment with UN recognition, this intervention allowed Turkey to secure a strategic maritime border in the Mediterranean and establish military bases in North Africa.

The Palestinian Arena

Breaking from its historical policy of balancing relations between Israelis and Palestinians, the AKP has shifted to explicitly supporting Hamas. By leveraging the argument that Hamas was democratically elected in 2006, Turkey masks its Islamist alignment behind democratic legitimacy, enabling it to host operatives and provide diplomatic backing.
